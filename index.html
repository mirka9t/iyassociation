Who We Are

A student organisation, built by students.

Here is the simple version: three friends noticed that clever, motivated classmates had nowhere to use that energy. So we made a place for it.

International Youth Association (IYA) was founded in 2026 at Polyglot International School in Samarkand, Uzbekistan. We organise events where students do real work: research a topic, stand up and speak about it, listen to people who disagree, and write down what everyone agreed on.

We are starting with one Model UN conference inside our own school. Step by step, we want to open it to our city, our country, and then further. You can see the full plan lower down this page.

The Basics

What is Model United Nations?

If you have never heard of it, do not worry. Almost everyone starts here.

Model UN is a role-play of a real United Nations meeting. Each student becomes a delegate — you represent one country, not yourself.

Your committee gets one real world problem to solve. You read about your country first, so you know what it thinks. Then everyone takes turns speaking, forms groups with countries that agree with them, and writes a resolution — a short document with the solutions the room can accept. At the end, everyone votes on it.

It is done in English, with simple rules for who speaks and when. Nobody expects you to be perfect. Most delegates are nervous in their first session and fine by the second.

On The Day

What actually happens at IYAMUN.

Four simple stages, from the moment you walk in to the moment you go home.

01

Opening

Everyone gathers, we explain the rules of the day, and you meet the students chairing your committee.

02

Debate

Your committee opens. Delegates take turns at the microphone and explain what their country wants.

03

Writing

Countries that agree work together and write a resolution — the shared list of solutions. Then the room votes.

04

Closing

We close the conference, hand out awards to standout delegates, and take a group photo.

Choose Your Room

The three committees.

A committee is simply the room you sit in and the kind of problem you discuss. You choose your preference when you apply.

General Assembly

The biggest room, where every country has a seat. You discuss wide topics such as education, climate, or development. Debate moves at a steady pace, so this is the friendliest place to start — most first-time delegates choose it.

Security Council

A small room of about fifteen delegates dealing with urgent peace and safety situations. Things move fast and you speak often. Best if you have debated before, or if you like pressure.

Human Rights Council

The room for questions about people: freedom, fairness, safety, and dignity. You balance what your country believes against what people everywhere need. Good for delegates who like writing and careful argument.

Why Join

What you get out of it.

Not certificates for a folder — skills you will actually use.

Speaking in public

You will stand up and talk to a room of people you do not know. The first time is hard. After that it stops being frightening — and that lasts for life.

Real responsibility

Members help run the conference itself — planning, research, chairing, logistics. You are not just a guest at someone else's event.

A network that grows

You meet students from your school now, your city next year, and further after that — as IYA moves up the roadmap, so does the circle you belong to.

Writing that persuades

Resolutions have to be clear and short, or nobody votes for them. That is the same skill behind good essays, applications, and emails.

Founding-year status

IYA is new. Early members shape the traditions, the committees, and the rules that everyone after them will follow.

FAQ

Questions people ask us.

What can I apply for right now?

Only IYAMUN on Sunday, 27 September 2026. General IYA membership and the later conferences are not open yet — they open in the order shown in the roadmap above.

Do I need experience?

No. Most people applying have never done Model UN before. We explain the rules on the day, and the chairs help you during the session. Curiosity matters more than experience.

Is my English good enough?

Almost certainly yes. The conference runs in English, but nobody is judged on accent or perfect grammar — only on whether your idea is clear. Simple, short sentences work very well in debate.

Do I have to be at Polyglot International School?

For this first conference we are starting inside Polyglot. From the next stage we open to other schools in Samarkand, so apply anyway and we will contact you when your stage opens.

What happens after I apply?

We read every application and reply to you directly with your committee and what to prepare before the day.

Does it cost anything?

Yes — IYAMUN 2026 has an application fee of 100,000 UZS per delegate. It covers your committee materials, the venue, and awards, and we will confirm the payment details once your application is reviewed.

Model UN words, in plain English

Delegate — you. At the conference you speak for a country, not for yourself.

Committee — the room you are placed in, and the kind of problem it discusses.

Chair — the student running your room. They decide who speaks next and keep the time.

Resolution — the short document your committee writes together, listing the solutions it agrees on. The room votes on it at the end.

Point / Motion — the polite phrases used to ask a question or to suggest what the room does next. The chairs teach these at the start, so you do not need to learn them now.

Position — what your country thinks about the topic. You research it before the day so you can argue like that country would.
